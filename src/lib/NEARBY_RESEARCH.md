# Neighborhood preview research

Reviewed October 13, 2026. This is editorial preview data, not a live places feed or an assurance that a business remains open. Recheck specific places and transit before public rollout and on a regular content-review cycle. No walking time or route distance is claimed.

Directions audit (October 13): All 106 OSM-backed named-place cards across the 17 Neighborhood datasets now retain coordinates from their linked OSM object. Their straight-line distance from the matched property address is under 750 m; an automated guard prevents a missing or distant source pin. Where the OSM record has a street number and road, the Google directions query includes the business name and full address, with the address visible on the card. Otherwise the link uses the source coordinate. This avoids branch-name searches and the adjacent-business snapping seen when Google was given only a coordinate (H Mart's point selected Sturgeon Bio NY; Duane Reade's point selected a Citibank ATM). The property origin remains a street address so Google shows a recognizable starting point; 61 Gold St is qualified as Manhattan because an unqualified geocoder result also exists in Brooklyn. The First Avenue Duane Reade uses the Google-checked 399 E 72nd St address, Krave Cafe & Deli uses 1399 2nd Ave, and Café Maud uses 1640 2nd Ave. The 17 transit cards now use nearby named access intersections rather than ambiguous station names ("116 St (6)" resolved to a street number); Google walking routes were checked for representative Lexington, Eighth Avenue, Fulton, and First Avenue/Q access. These are intersection waypoints, not verified individual station entrances or platform-level accessible routes.

- `nearby-highlights.json`: exact-address matches were checked with OpenStreetMap Nominatim; named places were selected from live OpenStreetMap search results within roughly 650 m straight-line of the address match. Each fact retains its OSM object link. Subway stations/complexes were matched to [MTA Subway Stations and Complexes](https://data.ny.gov/Transportation/MTA-Subway-Stations-and-Complexes/5f5g-n3cz); the Flushing and East Tremont pages use active stops from [MTA Bus Stops](https://data.ny.gov/Transportation/MTA-Bus-Stops/2ucp-7wg5). Station-center and stop coordinates are not station entrances or walking routes.
- `retail-nearby-anchors.json`: hotel, theater, and parking candidates were checked in the same map source and reviewed for relevance to the retail setting. These are nearby context, not proven customer sources or guaranteed parking availability.
- `retail-pedestrian-data.json`: all 114 NYC DOT [Bi-Annual Pedestrian Count](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Transportation/Bi-Annual-Pedestrian-Counts/cqsj-cfgu) locations were distance-ranked against the exact-address map match. The latest populated midday field at review was May 2026. The stored distance is approximate straight-line meters to DOT's counter point, not a walking route. A count is published only within 250 m and outside bridge counters; all other nearest matches are retained internally as `withheld_distance`. Even a published counter measures the named corridor, **not the storefront**. The [pedestrian sensor inventory](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Transportation/Bicycle-and-Pedestrian-Count-Sensors/6up2-gnw8) was also checked; no pedestrian-capable sensor was near these properties. [Pedestrian Demand](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Transportation/Pedestrian-Mobility-Plan-Pedestrian-Demand/fwpa-qxaf) is a street classification, not a measured count; no classification was published without an address-to-segment match.

Do not infer foot traffic, demographics, permitted uses, customer demand, or business suitability from these surrounding amenities or nearby-corridor counts. Refresh facts before changing `publication_status` or lifting the preview gate.
